# Retained-pivot validation and PR #1372 review split

## Decisions

[Hiroshi's scope decision](https://github.com/tensor4all/tensor4all-rs/pull/1372#issuecomment-6971978530)
separates retained-pivot validation/seeding/verdicts (PR A, #1352) from the
local-truncation and recovery experiment (draft PR B, #1371). PR A keeps the
local LU threshold unchanged. Its [stopping and bounded-history contract](../design/treetci-termination.md)
protects the largest observed residual witness and checks current retained
pivot pairs independently of the historical budget.

Equal maximum ranks do not establish a stationary approximation. An early
uncapped floor requires unchanged swept pivot sets; otherwise an unresolved run
may reach its iteration limit with an honest recorded error. Resource pressure
limits historical coverage rather than forcing a termination.

[Hiroshi's compatibility clarification](https://github.com/tensor4all/tensor4all-rs/issues/1371#issuecomment-6973070577)
accepts an opt-in Julia-compatible stop for PR B. Defaults keep the recorded
sampled maximum and refuse convergence above tolerance. API spelling waits for
the post-seeding cost comparison, including the 60-site slow-decay comb and the
bounded-recovery noise test. This does not expand PR A.

## Verification conclusions and constraints

The old hosted failures are deterministic: rerunning `5d40fd03` repeats the
Linux merge failure and both macOS failures. [Hiroshi independently reproduces
the merge failure](https://github.com/tensor4all/tensor4all-rs/pull/1372#issuecomment-6973145117).
An isolated original-LU/no-recovery ablation changes the chirp fixture from
21 leaves with five valid rank-two merges to 16 leaves with no merges, while
keeping the merge cap at three. Both approximations meet the original L2
allowance. This identifies the numerical experiment as sufficient to change
that fixture's no-merge assumption. PR A restores the original threshold and
passes the existing merge assertions; it does not change that fixture or its
accuracy threshold. An opt-in numerical experiment can legitimately change
the partition and must validate its own merge behavior separately.

The original [18-run experiment and hashes](https://github.com/tensor4all/tensor4all-rs/blob/5d40fd03b60d17a325af3ff0d8bf092b1e3de328/docs/worklogs/artifacts/2026-11-18-issue1371/README.md)
remain pinned evidence for `5d40fd03`, preserved for PR B. Its reproducer,
artifacts and Gaussian accuracy regression belong to that experiment. They are
not accuracy or performance claims about PR A. PR A retains the unchanged dense
#1352 regression thresholds and independent resource/verdict controls.

Original LU at tolerance 1e-6, seed 2 retains the published unsampled Gaussian
miss: recorded 9.93e-7 versus dense 1.018e-6. Sampled convergence is not a dense
error bound; PR A does not solve that #1371 numerical-policy limitation by
relaxing a tolerance or changing the local threshold.

The original PR A validation passed TreeTCI's unit and integration suites, the
unchanged #1352 dense comparisons, optimized numerical regressions, the downstream
partitioned unit/integration suites, affected doctests and Clippy. No numerical
assertion tolerance is relaxed. In the observed local runs, the 32-site seed-two
cases reach the requested 30 sweeps at both 32 MiB and 64 KiB, with eviction and
an above-tolerance recorded error, rather than a history-budget stop or false convergence. The
regression requires an unresolved result to complete all 30 sweeps, while still
allowing earlier below-tolerance convergence. It does not assert that every
provider returns exactly `MaxIterations`. The remaining numerical stall belongs
to PR B.

The two TreeTCI golden records keep their function evaluations, splits,
terminations, bond dimensions and node data. Cache hits become 31663 and 51349,
and a few error estimates change within 3.4e-16; only those intentional fields
are re-recorded. Dense-engine records and every comparison tolerance remain
unchanged. Hosted verification is required on the synchronized PR A head,
especially macOS because pivot trajectories differ across providers.

## Accuracy assertion repair

[The follow-up review](https://github.com/tensor4all/tensor4all-rs/pull/1372#issuecomment-7001671383)
identified that the reopened-table tests computed dense error but could accept
any inaccurate approximation reporting an above-tolerance sampled floor. The
fixed frozen-fixture checks require both `Converged` and the dense relative
residual at or below the existing tolerance. They retain the exact table,
initial pivots and the 1e-10 threshold. Both real and complex runs now cover
seeds 1–16: the previous focused complex seeds 4/6 are expanded in response to
[the independent review](https://github.com/tensor4all/tensor4all-rs/pull/1372#issuecomment-7002061471).
The older tables keep their seeds and default tolerance too.

A temporary negative control replaces seed three's materialized tensor with
zero and the verdict with `SampledErrorFloor`, with recorded error 2e-10. The
old assertion accepts the dense relative residual of one; the repaired
assertion rejects it at the accuracy check. Restoring the actual materialized
results passes all four frozen-fixture tests. The reopened-table runs all
converge locally. Simon's externally reported 5.1e-16 bound remains attributed
rerun evidence, not the CI assertion: the regression enforces the requested
1e-10 tolerance. Observed roundoff-level errors are not new assertion tolerances
or a bound on other target functions.

An honest sampled floor remains a valid production outcome. Its separate
restricted-cross test materializes the returned rank-one approximation to
`1 + i + j` and verifies the known dense maximum residual of one against the
recorded error. It covers a floor, an iteration limit, and disabled global
validation without letting any of them stand in for accurate reconstruction.

The uncapped floor keeps a window of three equal completed-sweep pivot
snapshots, hence two equality comparisons; the first
snapshot may differ from that sweep's input. Documentation and comments now
state that distinction explicitly. [Hiroshi's follow-up](https://github.com/tensor4all/tensor4all-rs/pull/1372#issuecomment-7001992441)
requires the 32-site floor arm to reject a three-sweep regression. Both uncapped
floor and iteration-limit outcomes now require all 30 requested sweeps and an
above-tolerance recorded residual. Below-tolerance convergence remains accepted;
a cap-caused floor is invalid because this test sets no cap. A second temporary
negative control injects an above-tolerance floor and a three-entry error
history: the old test accepts it and the repaired test rejects its premature
stop. These strengthened assertions keep the numerical tolerances unchanged.

## Zero-tolerance boundary and release follow-up

A sampled floor requires a positive residual even when `tolerance` is zero.
Strict convergence remains `error < tolerance`; a zero residual at zero tolerance
therefore reaches the iteration limit when uncapped, or the ordinary bond-cap
verdict when capped. A positive residual can still report a floor at zero
tolerance. The regression covers real/complex scalars, all five public entry
points, global checking on/off, and no cap, exact-rank cap and insufficient cap.
The restricted-cross positive-floor control also exercises zero tolerance.
No assertion tolerance is changed and local LU truncation remains unchanged.

The CHANGELOG records the changed non-converged verdicts and attributes the
7.4–8.4x oracle-count observation to Simon's specific smooth 60-site control.
[Tracking issue #1405](https://github.com/tensor4all/tensor4all-rs/issues/1405)
assigns the opt-in Julia-compatible follow-up to the PR author, with the
contract decision owned by Hiroshi, and sketches a `StopRule` enum without
promising a final API. The follow-up comparison will include small divisors
and adaptive tightening; the old-pin proxy is not a measurement
of that future implementation. The default sampled-error contract stays in
force, and PR A does not implement the opt-in local-budget or stopping policy.
